1934: SERA.8005 Operation of air traffic control service
1935: (a) In order to provide air traffic control service, an air traffic control unit shall:
1936: (1) be provided with information on the intended movement of each aircraft, or variations therefrom, and with
1937: current information on the actual progress of each aircraft;
1938: (2) determine from the information received, the relative positions of known aircraft to each other;
1939: (3) issue clearances and information for the purpose of preventing collision between aircraft under its control and of
1940: expediting and maintaining an orderly flow of traffic;
1941: (4) coordinate clearances as necessary with other units:
1942: (i) whenever an aircraft might otherwise conflict with traffic operated under the control of such other units;
1943: (ii) before transferring control of an aircraft to such other units.
1944: (b) Clearances issued by air traffic control units shall provide separation:
1945: (1) between all flights in airspace Classes A and B;
1946: (2) between IFR flights in airspace Classes C, D and E;
1947: (3) between IFR flights and VFR flights in airspace Class C;
1948: (4) between IFR flights and special VFR flights;
1949: (5) between special VFR flights unless otherwise prescribed by the competent authority;
1950: except that, when requested by the pilot of an aircraft and agreed by the pilot of the other aircraft and if so prescribed
1951: by the competent authority for the cases listed under b) above in airspace Classes D and E, a flight may be cleared
1952: subject to maintaining own separation in respect of a specific portion of the flight below 3 050 m (10 000 ft) during
1953: climb or descent, during day in visual meteorological conditions.

1963: (c) Except for cases when a reduction in separation minima in the vicinity of aerodromes can be applied, separation by an
1964: air traffic control unit shall be obtained by at least one of the following:
1965: (1) vertical separation, obtained by assigning different levels selected from the table of cruising levels in Appendix 3
1966: to the Annex to this Regulation, except that the correlation of levels to track as prescribed therein shall not apply
1967: whenever otherwise indicated in appropriate aeronautical information publications or air traffic control clearances.
1968: The vertical separation minimum shall be a nominal 300 m (1 000 ft) up to and including FL 410 and a nominal
1969: 600 m (2 000 ft) above this level;
1970: (2) horizontal separation, obtained by providing:
1971: (i) longitudinal separation, by maintaining an interval between aircraft operating along the same, converging or
1972: reciprocal tracks, expressed in time or distance; or
1973: (ii) lateral separation, by maintaining aircraft on different routes or in different geographical areas.
