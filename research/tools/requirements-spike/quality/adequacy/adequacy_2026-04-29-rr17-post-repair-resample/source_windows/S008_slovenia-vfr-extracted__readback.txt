524: ◊ Read back
525: Reading back a clearance and any safety critical information helps both the pilot and the
526: controller understand what the aircraft has been instructed to do. It also serves as a check
527: that the right aircraft, and only that aircraft, will take action on the clearance. It will be
528: easier for the pilot to read back the clearance if he notes it down.

552: When a read back is required, the pilot must make sure it is complete and finish it with the
553: call sign. It is normally best to read back the items in the order given. If the departure
554: instructions are transmitted together with a take‐off clearance, it is more appropriate to
555: read back the take‐off clearance first, followed by the departure instructions.
556: ◊ Use of ′wilco′
557: ′WILCO′ means ′I understand your message and will comply with it′. This standard phrase
558: should not be used instead of a full read back of the items from the previous paragraph.
559: ◊ Acknowledgement by call sign
560: If a transmission contains information that does not need to be read back, the pilot should
561: acknowledge it by transmitting his call sign or by transmitting his call sign preceded by the
562: word ′ROGER′.
563: ◊ Transmitting technique
564: Transmissions shall be conducted concisely in a normal conversational tone.
