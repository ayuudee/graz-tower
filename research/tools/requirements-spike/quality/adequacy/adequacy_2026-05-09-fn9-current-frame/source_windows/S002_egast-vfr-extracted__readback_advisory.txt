579: ‘Wilco’ means ‘I understand your message and will comply’. It should not be used

587: Phraseology
588:
589: Reading back a clearance and any safety critical information helps both the pilot
590: and the controller understand what the aircraft has been instructed to do. It also
591: serves as a check that the right aircraft, and only that aircraft, will take action on
592: the clearance. It may help to note down the clearance prior to read back and ask
593: any other pilots (or an educated passenger) in the aircraft to listen to all clearances,
594: including taxi clearance. If in any doubt you must request clarification.

601: Use of ‘Wilco’
602:
603: When a read back is required you must ensure it is complete and follow it with
604: your callsign. It is normally best to read back the items in the order given, as this
605: makes it easier for the controller to check the accuracy and identify any missing
606: items. However, there are some exceptions to this, e.g. if departure instructions are
607: transmitted together with a take-off clearance, it may be more logical to read back
608: the take-off clearance first, followed by the departure instructions.
